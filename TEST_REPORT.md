# Test report

Generated by `python -m eval.run_eval` (backend/). Every case runs end to end (parsing → matching → decision → database) on a fresh database with a fixed clock. Image cases run the real OCR pipeline on the three real slips and degraded copies of them.

Ground truth is what a careful person with the bank statement would conclude: **GENUINE** (should be accepted), **INVALID** (must not be accepted), **UNCERTAIN** (the evidence cannot decide; review is right).

## Headline

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Cases | 53 (10 with real OCR) |
| **False approvals** (INVALID or UNCERTAIN → APPROVED) | **0** |
| False rejections (GENUINE → REJECTED) | 0 |
| Wrong matches (approved against the wrong bank SMS) | 0 |
| Manual-review rate (NEEDS_VERIFICATION) | 49% (26) |
| Genuine payments approved automatically | 78% (18/23) |
| Exactly the ideal outcome | 70% |
| Crashes | 0 |

**Reading the review rate.** The case set is deliberately adversarial: only 23 of 53 cases are genuine payments, the rest are fraud or broken evidence. The review rate of real traffic will be far lower. The number that matters for staff workload is the genuine row: 18 of 23 genuine payments were approved with no human; the others were photos too damaged to read, the real folded cash-slip photo (confidence just under the line), or a payment another customer had tried to claim first. Invalid slips that were not rejected outright went to review, never to approval: forensic signals (edited time, edited amount, pending status) hold a payment, they never decide it alone.

## Where the system was not ideal (and why that is acceptable or not)

- **W3** Slip amount edited to 50,000; bank credited 5,000: truth INVALID, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`SMS_CONFLICT`). The slip contradicts the bank SMS; a person checks for editing.
- **A2** Different account read with low OCR confidence: truth INVALID, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`ACCOUNT_UNCERTAIN`). Wrong-looking account read with low confidence: a person checks instead of rejecting on an OCR error.
- **R4** Real payer submits after a fraudster's rejected attempt: truth GENUINE, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`SUSPICIOUS`). Money matched, but a warning signal (e.g. the same transaction was earlier submitted by another customer) means a person should decide who really paid.
- **R5** Same customer uses one payment for two orders: truth INVALID, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`SAME_CUSTOMER_OTHER_ORDER`). Same customer, two orders, one payment: a person asks which order it is for.
- **O2** Old Flash receipt (only one date on slip): truth INVALID, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`OLD_PAYMENT_UNCONFIRMED`). Only one date on this slip type, so an OCR misread could fake 'old'; a person confirms before rejecting.
- **E1** Time edited 16:18 -> 16:25: truth INVALID, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`SMS_CONFLICT`). The slip contradicts the bank SMS; a person checks for editing.
- **E2** Known reference with edited amount (other customer): truth INVALID, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`REFERENCE_REUSED_DIFFERENT_DETAILS`). Very likely an edited slip; held for a person (forensic signals never reject alone).
- **E3** Receipt 'generated' a day before the transfer: truth INVALID, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`SUSPICIOUS`). Money matched, but a warning signal (e.g. the same transaction was earlier submitted by another customer) means a person should decide who really paid.
- **E4** Date edited forward one day (future): truth INVALID, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`FUTURE_DATED`). Impossible time: held for a person rather than auto-rejected, in case of an OCR misread.
- **E5** Screenshot of a PENDING transfer: truth INVALID, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`NOT_COMPLETED`). Not completed transfers are held (not rejected) because the transfer may still complete.
- **E6** Screenshot of a SCHEDULED transfer: truth INVALID, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`NOT_COMPLETED`). Not completed transfers are held (not rejected) because the transfer may still complete.
- **E7** Cash slip date differs from the SMS date: truth INVALID, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`SMS_CONFLICT`). The slip contradicts the bank SMS; a person checks for editing.
- **I3** Real People's Bank cash-slip PHOTO (rotated, folded): truth GENUINE, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`LOW_CONFIDENCE`). The SMS matched, but OCR confidence on the photo was below the auto-approve line; the SMS is held for this order and staff approve with one click. Deliberate: prefer review over a risky approval.
- **I7** Real e-receipt heavily blurred: truth GENUINE, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`UNREADABLE_SLIP`). OCR could not read the critical fields; the customer is asked for a clearer slip and staff can type the values.
- **I9** Real cash slip, low resolution (25%): truth GENUINE, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`UNREADABLE_SLIP`). OCR could not read the critical fields; the customer is asked for a clearer slip and staff can type the values.
- **I10** Real e-receipt cropped to top 30% (no time): truth GENUINE, got **NEEDS_VERIFICATION** (`UNREADABLE_SLIP`). OCR could not read the critical fields; the customer is asked for a clearer slip and staff can type the values.

## Other automated tests

The pytest suite (`python -m pytest` in backend/) additionally covers: 64 single-field mutation tests (any changed amount/minute/date/account digit/reference on an approved slip is never approved, including when resubmitted by another customer), a forced-interleaving race test proving two orders cannot both claim one SMS, 18 image-degradation tests asserting OCR is never *confidently wrong*, webhook security (bad signature, replay, non-bank sender), staff actions, payment windows and refunds.
